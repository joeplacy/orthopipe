The strategic read

Your core bet is validated — but the wedge is narrower and sharper than “automation.”

Scan → print automation is table stakes: Materialise Phits, Aetrex/SOLS, HP Arize, and Denmark’s Sequence all ship it, some with pressure-plate fusion and “reorder in three clicks.” Competing on “we automate design” walks straight into incumbents.

The genuinely defensible combination — which no competitor pairs together — is: (1) LLM-parsed free-text prescriptions (every rival uses fixed forms), (2) a deterministic, range-clamped, human-approved geometry engine with a full audit trail, and (3) per-zone variable-stiffness lattice interiors driven by that prescription — which the newest peer-reviewed work shows cut peak plantar pressure 36–42% versus uniform infill.

Reframe the pitch from “we automate insole design” to “we turn a prescription sentence into a validated, pressure-optimized, printable part with a traceable record from words to STL.” That is a claim about trust and clinical intent, not just throughput — and it is the one thing the incumbents’ form-driven, black-box pipelines can’t easily copy.

03 · Academic findings that should change the engine

What the evidence says to build

Your current engine is a heightfield shell with a flat bottom. The literature points somewhere specific: a shell wrapping a zoned, variable-stiffness lattice interior — and it warns against a few assumptions worth un-learning early.

build this

Zoned lattices beat uniform infill — measurably

A partition-infilled TPMS insole cut peak plantar pressure ~36% (static) and ~42% (dynamic gait) versus a uniform gyroid insole, with 30–37% lower mean pressure in gait.

Implication: the single biggest product lever. Move from “shell + flat bottom” to “shell + per-zone lattice.” This is the differentiator, and it’s backed by hard numbers.
default map

A concrete zone → lattice mapping

Stiffness ranks Diamond > Gyroid > Primitive at equal parameters. The paper assigns soft Primitive to the heel (deform, spread contact), Gyroid to the forefoot, and stiff Diamond to toe + midfoot (load-bearing).

Implication: bake this as the default per-zone map in apply_mods; let the Rx nudge it. You inherit a validated starting point instead of guessing.
design rule

Blend stiffness smoothly, don’t step it

Graded TPU gyroids last longest under fatigue with moderate, smooth gradients; abrupt stiffness jumps between zones degrade fatigue life (cushioning study — extrapolated to insoles).

Implication: interpolate lattice density across Rx zone boundaries. Sharp zone edges are a durability failure mode — the same instinct as your existing min-thickness clamp.
don’t hardcode

Gyroid isn’t automatically best

In TPU specimens, honeycomb gave the highest compression load at 50% strain and the most energy absorption; gyroid was the lowest.

Implication: make lattice family a parameter in the schema, not a hardcoded gyroid. Different zones and indications want different cells.
reality check

Bench stiffness ≠ in-gait pressure

Honeycomb vs gyroid at 20% infill showed no significant in-shoe pressure difference vs no insole in one walking study; a separate n=21 pilot saw increased metatarsal pressure and no FFI gain.

Implication: geometric “validation pass” isn’t clinical proof. Add a pressure-based check (simulated or plate-fused) before claiming efficacy — and it becomes a review-UI edge over Phits.
positioning

“3D-printed” is not automatically better

A pilot RCT favored traditionally fabricated orthoses over HP Arize at 8 weeks (PROMIS function 49.0 vs 41.9). A review agrees 3D-printed can win on fit/comfort but evidence is thin.

Implication: don’t sell “3D-printed = superior.” Compete on speed, cost, traceability, and pressure-optimization. Let the audit trail and validation carry the credibility.
04 · US regulatory footing

Exempt, but not unregulated

Good news up front: custom foot orthotics are generally Class I and 510(k)-exempt, so there’s no premarket submission. The nuance is where new founders trip.

What holds

  • 510(k)-exempt, Class I. No premarket notification for the device itself.
  • GMP-lite. Exempt from most of 21 CFR 820 except recordkeeping & complaint files (§820.35). Your append-only audit log is already the right instinct — extend it to complaint handling.
  • You are “patient-matched,” not “custom.” FDA treats scan-fit devices made within cleared specs as patient-matched → normal (exempt) path, not the Custom Device Exemption.

Where it bites

  • Don’t lean on the Custom Device Exemption. §520(b) caps it at 5 units/yr per device type — useless for volume.
  • Central fabrication = you’re a manufacturer. Registration + GMP-lite + MDR reporting apply. Only dispensing-only retail O&P is registration-exempt (§807.65(i)) — that isn’t you.
  • Confirm the exact product code. §890.3025 (accessories) does not classify a foot orthosis; the real code is a shoe-insert / arch-support classification. Nail this before any claims.

The PHI angle on your Rx parser

Prescriptions can carry protected health information. Sending them to a cloud LLM raises a privacy/BAA question. Two mitigations, both verified viable: sign a BAA for the Claude API, and/or ship a local-LLM fallback — a local Llama-70B extracted explicit clinical features from free-text notes at 95–100% sensitivity, so on-prem parsing is realistic when a site forbids cloud calls.

Not legal advice. Confirm the device’s FDA product code and talk to a regulatory consultant before market entry — these are the load-bearing items, and the exact classification is the one open question the research couldn’t close.

Method & open questions

How this was built — and what’s still open

Method. Two fan-out research passes: parallel web search across five angles, source fetching & claim extraction, then 3-vote adversarial verification (a claim survives only if it isn’t refuted). 34 findings cleared the bar; every competitor, license, number, and regulation here passed. Claims that failed — e.g. a competitor’s export formats, an unverifiable scanner-ecosystem detail — were dropped.

Confidence. Competitor facts rest on vendor pages corroborated by App Store / trade press — solid for scope, but “three clicks” and self-reported scan counts are vendor best-case. Academic numbers are from the cited papers directly.
